### Chapter 1 – The Search for Truth

I don't remember deciding to search for truth.

I only remember realizing I couldn't stop.

When I was seventeen, I started carrying a notebook with me wherever I went. It wasn't filled with poetry or stories. It was filled with questions.

Questions about God.

Questions about life.

Questions about why the world could be so beautiful one moment and so broken the next.

Looking back, I realize that notebook became the beginning of a lifelong journey.

### The Early Search

At first, I assumed truth would be easy to find.

Surely someone already had the answers.

Surely there was one teacher, one book, one church, one philosophy that could explain everything.

Instead, every answer seemed to produce three more questions.

Rather than discouraging me, it pulled me deeper.

I read Scripture.

I read philosophy.

I read science.

I listened more than I spoke.

People sometimes mistook my questions for rebellion.

They weren't.

I wasn't trying to tear anything down.

I was trying to understand.

### Faith and Thinking

One thing became clear very early in my life.

I wasn't interested in blind faith.

Neither was I interested in knowledge without purpose.

I wanted both.

A faith that welcomed difficult questions.

A mind that wasn't afraid of mystery.

For years one quotation stayed in the back of my mind:

"An educated man should know something about everything, and everything about something."

Whether I knew its source or not, it became a personal compass.

It reminded me that learning wasn't about collecting information.

It was about becoming a more complete human being.

### The Journey

That search eventually carried me into places I never expected.

Prison.

Churches.

Corporate boardrooms.

Technology.

Farming.

Business.

Friendships.

Failure.

Success.

Every season taught me something the previous one could not.

Truth wasn't confined to one building, one profession, or one chapter of life.

It revealed itself in unexpected places.
