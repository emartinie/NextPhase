### Rebuilding – From Ashes to Blueprint

Rebuilding didn’t start with hammers or blueprints—it started in my heart. After the farm was stripped bare and my possessions reduced to a storage unit’s worth of memories, it would have been easy to just walk away. But covenant love—first with God, then with Amy—gave me the courage to look at the ruins and say, This is not the end.

Amy and I spent those months traveling, staying in Airbnbs, piecing together what was left of my life. We laughed, we prayed, we made love, we healed. Every trip back to the farm was a reminder that I hadn’t lost the vision; it was just waiting for a new foundation to be laid.

When you’ve been betrayed by partners and abandoned by friends, you learn quickly who you are without them. You also learn what’s worth fighting for. For me, the farm was more than land—it was a sanctuary, a place where people could gather, learn, and heal. It had been tarnished, but its purpose wasn’t gone.

I started small—cleaning up what was left, hauling debris, putting order to chaos. Each task felt like a declaration: You can take my things, but you can’t take my calling. Slowly, plans took shape. Amy’s presence reminded me that rebuilding is not just about property; it’s about the people who believe in you when the world thinks you’re done.

The fish came back. The garden started again. The grow rooms lit up like hope itself. Families began to fish at the ponds, laughter returned to the tournaments, and even with fewer hands to help, I felt stronger than ever because I wasn’t doing it for money—I was doing it for purpose.

Rebuilding wasn’t instant, but it was relentless. It was covenant in action: faithfulness to a vision that refused to die.
