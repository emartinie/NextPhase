### Chapter: July 20,2026- The Day My Bank Account Went to Negative Fifteen Thousand Dollars

There are days that don't simply test your patience.

They test your identity.

One of those days began with a bank account that had suddenly dropped to nearly fifteen thousand dollars below zero.

It wasn't because I had gone on a spending spree. It wasn't because I had made some reckless financial decision. For years, my child support had been taken directly from every paycheck through an Income Withholding Order. I had lived with that deduction for so long that it had simply become another part of life.

My son had just turned eighteen.

When I contacted child support, I was told the payroll deductions would probably continue until the end of the month. That made sense to me. It wasn't what I wanted to hear, but at least it was understandable.

Then everything changed.

Without warning, my bank account was levied.

The money that was actually in my account—around two thousand dollars that I needed for bills and survival—became inaccessible. Overnight, the account reflected a balance approaching negative fifteen thousand dollars.

It wasn't just a number on a screen.

It was a message.

Another reminder that no matter how hard I worked, another crisis always seemed to arrive before I had a chance to catch my breath.

As if that weren't enough, work had just announced mandatory unpaid time off. Income was already shrinking.

The same day, I was dealing with another problem that carried its own weight. My truck—the one I depended on every day—was tied up in warranty issues after an engine replacement. My brother-in-law had stepped in and paid for that engine when I couldn't, and I had committed to paying him back one thousand dollars every month until the debt was gone.

I wasn't trying to escape my responsibilities.

I was trying to honor every one of them.

Every direction I turned, someone had to be paid.

Every decision felt like choosing which fire to put out first.

Looking back, what stays with me isn't the dollar amount.

It isn't the paperwork.

It isn't even the frustration.

It's the exhaustion.

There comes a point when a person stops asking, "Why is this happening?" and starts asking, "How many more hits can one season contain?"

Those are dangerous moments.

Not because they destroy your finances.

Because they tempt you to believe they have destroyed your future.

Yet somehow, life kept moving.

I still went to work.

I still answered the phone.

I still paid what I could.

I still kept building HomeGroups.

I still kept studying.

I still kept believing that God was writing a story much larger than the chapter I happened to be living.

That day wasn't really about child support.

It was about discovering that perseverance isn't proven when life is fair.

It's proven when life isn't.

Years from now, I doubt I'll remember the exact balance on that bank account.

But I'll remember the lesson.

Money can disappear.

Plans can unravel.

Another unexpected letter can arrive in the mail.

But character is built in those moments when you continue moving forward anyway.

### Reflection

If God places a calling within each of us, then purpose is not about becoming someone else.

It is about becoming the person He created us to be.

The journey is rarely straight.

It often includes failure, disappointment, unexpected detours, and seasons of waiting.

But none of those things are wasted.

Each one becomes another thread in the story God is writing.
