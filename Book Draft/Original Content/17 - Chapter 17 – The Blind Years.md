### Chapter 17 – The Blind Years

There are seasons when you don't realize you're changing until years later.

I call those years the blind years.

Not because I couldn't see.

Because I didn't yet have the language to describe what I was seeing.

### Reading Without Assumptions

Prison changed the way I read Scripture.

For the first time in my life, I wasn't surrounded by sermons, church traditions, or endless opinions telling me what the text was supposed to mean.

I read slowly.

Carefully.

Sometimes only a few verses at a time.

I wasn't trying to prove a doctrine.

I simply wanted to understand what the biblical authors were actually saying.

I found myself praying something unexpected:

"Lord, don't let me lose this closeness when I leave here."

That prayer stayed with me long after I walked out of prison.

### Something Didn't Fit

As the years passed, one realization kept returning.

The Jesus I was reading about felt familiar...

...yet different.

His words weren't different.

The framework was.

He spoke naturally about covenant.

About Torah.

About Israel.

About obedience.

About the Scriptures.

Somehow those themes felt much more central than I remembered hearing in church.

I couldn't explain it.

I only knew something didn't quite fit together.

### Learning New Language

Years later I began discovering that other people had been asking similar questions.

I encountered historical research, Jewish scholarship, and discussions about the first-century context of Scripture.

For the first time I had vocabulary for ideas that had been growing quietly for years.

It wasn't that my faith had changed overnight.

It was that I finally had words for questions I had been carrying for a long time.

### Following the Evidence

That season taught me an important lesson.

I didn't want to become someone who defended traditions simply because they were familiar.

Neither did I want to reject them simply because they were traditional.

I wanted to follow the evidence wherever it led.

Sometimes that confirmed what I already believed.

Sometimes it challenged me.

Either way, I considered the pursuit worthwhile.

Truth isn't threatened by honest investigation.

### Reflection

Looking back, I no longer see those years as a period of confusion.

They were years of preparation.

God wasn't giving me all the answers.

He was teaching me how to ask better questions.

And sometimes...

Learning how to ask the right question is the beginning of finding the right answer.
