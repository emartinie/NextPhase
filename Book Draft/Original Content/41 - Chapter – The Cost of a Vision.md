### Chapter – The Cost of a Vision

The farm was not just land; it was a stage, a sanctuary, and a battleground all in one. From the moment I first stepped onto the property, I saw more than an overpriced catfish operation with Saturday tournaments and fried pork sandwiches—I saw a platform where every dream, every brand, and every calling could converge.

It took three years to make it mine.

Three years of walking the property, learning every inch of it, and enduring the circus of reality TV pilots being filmed by Lanny Parkhurst and his partner, Gary Wayne Bridges. They were smooth talkers, living in their own show business world. No one there knew who I really was, or the connections I had.

When I finally made an offer, Lanny rejected it.

I drove to the farm anyway. We sat on the BigWhiskers porch, and he explained that my bid had stirred up the producers. They thought I would ruin their production schedule. I told him flatly, “I don’t care about your production. We can protect whatever needs protecting. But you need to know this deal is real.”

On speakerphone, Gary Bridges boasted about a stage and funding from Gibson Guitars. I listened silently, then asked Lanny if he was sure. After hearing his sales pitch, I finally said, “You know how I know there’s no deal with Gibson? Because I meet with the CEO every week. If there was any deal happening five miles from my house, I’d know about it.”

His jaw dropped. A week later, we were having lunch at Piccadilly, where I learned that Gary wasn’t just an Elvis impersonator—he had taken Elvis’ place and toured with the original band. The coincidences felt divine, and the negotiations shifted in my favor.

I told Lanny after a long meeting about invasive carp:

“That was hard to sit through. All I heard was people trying to get $50,000. So how about I give you five $50,000s, and you give me the farm?”

Less than a week later, we were signing papers.

Brands, Dreams, and Convictions

BigWhiskers became more than a business—it became a hub where all my ventures could connect. Heritage Financial, HomeGroups, MyAquaFarm, HeritageHemp, Kids2Kids Learning—each had a place in the larger plan. I envisioned tournaments, aquaponics systems, media productions, and a community thriving together.

But there was tension.

The farm’s money-makers were catfish and pork—things the Bible said were unclean. The tournaments happened on Saturdays—the Sabbath. Every success I built came with a spiritual cost, a question of whether I was building something holy or something compromised. Still, I pressed on, believing God had led me here for a reason.

The Fall

And then, one by one, the people I trusted left.

Lanny abandoned us.

Benny abandoned me.

Jerry wanted to partner—then he abandoned me.

Jay abandoned me.

And finally, Wendy abandoned me.

Each name fell like a stone in the quiet water of the farm’s lake.

I stood there alone, with only the vision God had given me, and the weight of everything I had built.
