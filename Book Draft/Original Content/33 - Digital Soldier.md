### Digital Soldier

Back in 2008, I created over fifty secret Facebook groups to share truth. At that time, even the people closest to me thought I was crazy.

I posted warnings about Agenda 21, the Federal Reserve, the coming dollar collapse, and corruption in both parties. I exposed the CIA’s role in funding groups like Al-Qaeda. Years later, when the mainstream media finally admitted pieces of what I had been saying, I was already labeled a conspiracy theorist.

But I didn’t stop.

I wasn’t posting for fame, or money.

I posted because God called me to be a watchman.

The work of digital soldiers was to plant seeds. Some would never sprout, but others would grow and shake entire nations.

Ezekiel 37: Dry Bones Rising

The HomeGroups logo—simple to most—was prophetic to me. The circle of houses represented more than community; it symbolized the valley of dry bones in Ezekiel 37.

God told Ezekiel to prophesy to those bones. As he spoke, they came together, bone to bone, covered in flesh, and finally breathed with the Spirit of God.

That’s what I saw happening—not just online, not just politically, but spiritually.

Dead faith was waking up.

The scattered church was being rebuilt, house by house, voice by voice.

HomeGroups.Org wasn’t just a platform—it was a prophecy.

Threads of the Supernatural

This spiritual warfare wasn’t abstract. It was real, tangible. I saw it in my marriage, in the voice that called my name at the cabin, in demonic encounters that left me shaken but resolute.

I wasn’t always sure what I believed about demons or oppression. But I didn’t need to have a neat theology to know what I experienced was real.

And I knew God wins.

Journals From the Edge

During the divorce, my journals turned darker but also sharper.

Revelations came like flashes:

•	Leadership is sacrifice, not control.

•	Intimacy begins with vulnerability, even when it’s not returned.

•	A man can fight for his marriage and still lose it, but he cannot fight and stay unchanged.

Those thoughts became my lifeline. I wrote not just to survive, but to remember.
