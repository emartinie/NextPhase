### Chapter 8 – The Vision Takes Root

There are moments when you realize something you've been building for years has never really been about the project itself.

That's what happened as the farm began to recover.

Big Whiskers had become much more than a business. It had become a place where people gathered, asked questions, shared meals, and discovered that meaningful conversations often happen around a pond or a campfire as naturally as they do inside a church building.

That's when I finally understood what HomeGroups was supposed to become.

### A Different Picture of Community

Long before I owned the farm, I had been developing the idea behind HomeGroups.

It wasn't born out of dissatisfaction with the church.

It was born out of a desire to rediscover something I believed the earliest believers experienced naturally.

People gathering in homes.

Sharing meals.

Studying Scripture together.

Praying together.

Living life together.

Faith wasn't intended to be something practiced for an hour on Sunday and then packed away until next week.

It was meant to become part of everyday life.

### The Farm Becomes the Classroom

What I had imagined for years suddenly became visible.

Families fished together.

Children played while adults talked.

People with very different backgrounds sat around the same table.

Some had spent their lives in church.

Others had walked away from it.

Some weren't sure what they believed.

It didn't matter.

Everyone was welcome to ask questions.

There were no membership requirements.

No pressure.

No debates to win.

Just honest conversations about Scripture, faith, life, and the God who invites people into covenant with Him.

### Returning to the Foundations

As I studied the Bible more deeply, I became increasingly convinced that many believers had inherited traditions they had never personally examined.

That realization didn't make me angry.

It made me curious.

I wanted to understand Scripture on its own terms.

Its covenants.

Its appointed times.

Its historical setting.

Its Jewish context.

The more I studied, the more those pieces seemed to fit together into one continuous story rather than two separate religions.

Those discoveries naturally became part of our conversations.

Not as arguments.

As invitations to keep learning.

### A Living Community

HomeGroups slowly became less of a website and more of a vision.

The technology was important.

The curriculum mattered.

But neither was the point.

People were.

The goal was never to build another organization.

It was to encourage genuine community where people could grow together, ask difficult questions, and pursue truth without fear.

### Looking Forward

The farm taught me something I hadn't fully understood before.

Buildings don't create community.

People do.

Technology doesn't create disciples.

Relationships do.

If HomeGroups succeeds, it won't be because of an app, a website, or a curriculum.

It will succeed because ordinary people choose to open their homes, share their lives, and walk together in pursuit of truth.
