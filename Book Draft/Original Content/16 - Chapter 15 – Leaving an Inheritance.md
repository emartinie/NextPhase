### Chapter 15 – Leaving an Inheritance

For most of my life, I wasn't trying to become wealthy.

I was trying to become the first.

The first person in my family to build something that would outlive me.

I didn't inherit money.

I didn't inherit land.

I didn't inherit a business waiting for me to take over.

No one handed me a blueprint and said, "Here's how you build a lasting legacy."

Everything I built started with empty hands.

### Breaking the Pattern

Growing up, I watched people work hard simply to survive.

Bills were paid.

Life moved forward.

But very little remained for the next generation.

I quietly made a promise to myself.

That pattern would end with me.

I wanted my children to begin life from a stronger foundation than I had.

Not because I wanted to make life easy for them.

Because I wanted to give them opportunities I never had.

### Every Brick Had a Purpose

As my career grew, I found myself thinking differently about almost everything I purchased.

A house wasn't simply a place to live.

It was part of my children's future.

The farm wasn't merely property.

It was something I hoped they would someday enjoy, protect, and perhaps build upon.

The tools in my workshop.

The greenhouse.

The ponds.

The businesses.

The equipment.

None of it felt temporary.

Each represented another brick in something I hoped would become an inheritance—not only of possessions, but of stability.

I wasn't building for today.

I was building for tomorrow.

### More Than Money

Over time I realized that inheritance means much more than assets.

Money disappears.

Property changes hands.

Businesses come and go.

Character lasts.

Faith lasts.

Wisdom lasts.

The greatest inheritance a parent leaves isn't found in a will.

It's found in the values children carry long after their parents are gone.

That realization slowly changed my priorities.

I still wanted to leave tangible resources for my children.

But I became even more determined to leave them an example worth following.

### A Father's Hope

Like every parent, I imagined the future.

I pictured my children standing where I once stood—but with a stronger foundation beneath them.

I hoped they would inherit more than my possessions.

I hoped they would inherit courage.

Curiosity.

Integrity.

A willingness to pursue truth wherever it leads.

If they could build something greater than I ever did, I would consider my life's work a success.

That has always been the goal.

Not that my children remember what I owned.

But that they remember who I tried to become.

### Reflection

Legacies aren't created in a single moment.

They are built one decision at a time.

Every sacrifice.

Every lesson.

Every act of love.

Every difficult season faithfully endured.

Those become the true inheritance we leave behind.

If my children receive anything from my life, I hope it isn't merely what I accumulated.

I hope it's the conviction that one generation really can change the story for the next.

### One Prayer

As I stood there, I closed my eyes and prayed the simplest prayer I knew.

"Not my will, but Thine be done."

I didn't pray it because I expected a miracle.

I didn't pray it because I thought the charges would disappear.

I prayed it because I had reached the end of myself.

Nothing changed in the courtroom.

No dramatic reversal.

No last-minute rescue.

I was sentenced.

Looking back, however, I now realize that while my circumstances remained the same, something inside me had already begun to change.

### Learning Time Again

Prison altered my relationship with time.

Outside, every day had been filled with appointments, deadlines, responsibilities, and distractions.

Inside, time slowed to a pace I had never experienced.

Hours stretched.

Days blended together.

Simple routines became the structure of life.

Meals.

Count.

Lights out.

Visitors.

Everything happened according to someone else's schedule.

At first it felt like punishment.

Eventually it became a gift.

For the first time in my life, I had uninterrupted space to think.

### Questions Worth Asking

Silence has a way of exposing questions you've successfully avoided.

Mine weren't merely about prison.

They were about life.

About purpose.

About God.

About truth.

Did truth exist independently of what people believed?

Could suffering have meaning?

Was obedience valuable even when it produced no visible reward?

Those questions became my companions.

I didn't emerge with all the answers.

I emerged with better questions.

### Remembering an Earlier Prayer

While reflecting on those years, I remembered something from childhood.

A small piece of paper had survived decades of moves, failures, successes, and even prison.

It recorded that as a young boy I had asked Jesus into my heart.

I don't remember that day.

But I do remember another prayer from later in childhood.

After hearing the story of Solomon, I prayed for wisdom.

Not success.

Not wealth.

Wisdom.

At the time I probably didn't understand what I was asking.

Today I recognize that wisdom often arrives through experiences we would never willingly choose.

### The Gift of Silence

Prison didn't become the defining chapter of my life.

It became the classroom.

The silence stripped away distractions.

It forced me to confront who I was apart from my career, possessions, accomplishments, and reputation.

Many people think freedom begins when prison ends.

For me, a different kind of freedom began while I was still behind those walls.

It began with surrender.

It grew through questions.

And it continues every time I choose truth over comfort.
