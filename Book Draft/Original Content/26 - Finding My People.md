### Finding My People

There was a season when I genuinely believed I was simply ahead of my time. I had spent years studying the Bible independently, connecting passages that I rarely heard discussed together, and questioning assumptions that seemed deeply embedded in modern Christianity. At the time, I didn't know anyone who viewed Scripture quite the way I did. It was both exciting and lonely.

Then something completely unexpected happened.

It had nothing to do with theology.

I was trying to build an aquaponics greenhouse in my backyard.

While researching aquaponics, I found a man named Travis. He was one of those rare people who had already spent years solving the problems I was just beginning to encounter. After several conversations, I purchased about $6,000 worth of equipment and components from him. I respected his expertise enormously.

Naturally, I assumed our relationship would be one-sided. He would teach me aquaponics, and perhaps I could return the favor by sharing some of my biblical research.

Instead, God humbled me.

As we talked, Travis began connecting biblical ideas that I had never fully assembled myself. He introduced me to concepts and communities I didn't even know existed. What I thought was a lonely journey suddenly opened into an entire world of believers who had been asking many of the same questions.

Some people would describe them as Messianic or Hebrew Roots believers. Those labels have never completely fit me, and they certainly don't capture the diversity within those communities. But for the first time, I discovered there were thousands of believers around the world who shared a similar desire: to understand the Scriptures as a unified story rather than as disconnected religious traditions.

That realization changed everything.

For years I had been told—sometimes directly and sometimes indirectly—that I was simply mistaken or eccentric. Suddenly I found myself surrounded by thoughtful, kingdom-minded people who loved the Scriptures deeply and were wrestling with many of the same questions I had been asking.

Those were some of the most exciting years of my life.

I traveled to conferences. I met authors whose books I had begun reading. I spent countless hours on the phone discussing Scripture with believers from around the world. Some of them later stopped by our home while traveling through Tennessee. Others invited me to dinner when we happened to be in the same city.

The conversations were remarkable.

There was very little interest in defending denominations or preserving traditions. Instead, there was a shared desire to sharpen one another through honest study. It truly felt like Proverbs 27:17 in action:

"As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."

One unexpected blessing came much closer to home.

During that season my wife had returned after a painful separation. Watching her attend these gatherings and meet hundreds of believers who shared many of the convictions I had been trying to explain for years changed something for both of us. She could see with her own eyes that these ideas were not the product of one person's imagination. There was an international community wrestling with these same questions.

That mattered.

Around that same time I had released the first version of my Bible study app. To my surprise, several well-known churches reached out about building apps for them. Looking back, I probably wasn't ready for those opportunities. I was still carrying frustration toward much of mainstream Christianity, and that frustration undoubtedly influenced how I viewed those conversations.

What excited me far more was sharing ideas with people whose primary focus was the Kingdom of God rather than the preservation of institutions.

As I became more involved, I learned that leaders from many of these groups had gathered in Jerusalem for what they called their first international congress, an effort to build relationships among believers from many different countries. By the time I reached out, I had missed another gathering that had taken place only days or weeks earlier at Henry Horton State Park—ironically, one of the closest conference locations to my own home.

I had almost literally missed it by days.

When I asked where everyone would meet next, the answer was Florida.

My wife and I decided to go.

It was an unforgettable experience.

Hundreds of people gathered from many different backgrounds, cultures, and nations, united not by denominational identity but by a desire to understand and live the Scriptures more faithfully. Many of the friendships that shaped the next chapter of my life began during that gathering.

Some of those relationships continued for years.

A few people eventually wrote books and kindly mailed me signed copies with notes that said things like, "I think you'll recognize a few of these ideas." They weren't claiming the ideas were mine. They were simply acknowledging that our conversations had helped shape their thinking, just as their conversations had shaped mine.

That is exactly how healthy scholarship should work.

No one owns the pursuit of truth.

We simply sharpen one another along the way.

Recently I came across a project called Whole Bible Community. It reminded me of many of the conversations and ideas that circulated during those years. To be clear, I don't believe people build similar projects because they copied me. Good ideas often emerge independently when people are trying to solve similar problems.

What struck me instead was how much my own vision has continued to grow.

The HomeGroups platform has become far more than I imagined during those early gatherings. What began as a Bible study app has steadily evolved into a research platform, a teaching platform, and—hopefully—a place where believers from many different backgrounds can investigate Scripture together with intellectual honesty, humility, and a genuine desire to follow wherever the evidence leads.

Looking back, I no longer think of those years as the time when I found people who agreed with me.

I think of them as the years when God reminded me that I still had much to learn.

### The Farm Purchaser

During the years when I was teaching through Facebook groups, I experienced what I still consider one of the most meaningful seasons of my life. Thousands of people were following the conversations, but my focus was never on building an audience. My focus was on helping people learn how to study, how to ask better questions, and how to become disciples who could think for themselves.

One of those groups became especially close. We spent countless hours discussing Scripture, challenging assumptions, and encouraging one another to pursue truth with humility. Along the way, I began sharing a dream that had been growing in my heart for years.

I wanted to buy a farm.

Not simply to own land, but to build a community—a place where people could gather, learn, work together, and live out many of the ideas we had been discussing. At the time it was only a vision, and the farm itself had already become a long journey. I had spent nearly three years trying to purchase it from an owner who could never quite decide whether he actually wanted to sell.

Then something happened that I never could have planned.

One day, one of the people I cared about most in that community came to me and said, "The Lord put it on my heart to help you buy that farm."

I didn't know what to say.

My response wasn't yes or no. It was simple.

"If that's really what the Creator is leading you to do, then go back and pray about it. Make sure. I'll pray about it too."

Time passed.

Eventually she came back and said she had prayed, sought confirmation, and still believed this was what she was supposed to do.

I told her I still wasn't sure what that meant in practical terms and suggested she simply make a proposal.

The proposal was beyond anything I ever imagined.

She provided a quarter of a million dollars so I could purchase the farm and begin building the community we had been talking about.

Looking back, the financial gift was extraordinary, but it wasn't the whole story. The road to that farm had already taken years, and what followed would become another story altogether. In many ways, the purchase of the farm was not the end of one journey. It was the beginning of another.
