### Chapter: Love in the Ashes

When Amy came to the farm, she walked into more than just a property; she stepped into the remains of a dream. The ponds were still there, the Big Whiskers sign still swung in the breeze, but the life that once pulsed through the place—the laughter of families fishing, the smell of tournaments cooking under the sun—had dimmed. I was a man stripped bare, surrounded by memories that threatened to crush me.

But Amy didn’t come as a rescuer. She came as a companion.

The nights we spent together were the first nights in years where I felt seen, heard, and deeply loved. It wasn’t just physical; it was the kind of intimacy where your soul exhales after holding its breath for too long. She saw the scars Wendy had left, not just on my finances or possessions, but on my heart. And she didn’t flinch.

With her, I rediscovered what love was supposed to feel like:

•	Love that listens without judgment.

•	Love that gives without keeping score.

•	Love that heals, not just in words but in every quiet touch, every sunrise we watched together, every shared meal cooked in the small kitchen of a life that was rebuilding itself piece by piece.

For the first time since I could remember, I believed in soulmates again. Amy and I would spend long nights talking about everything—God, the world, our fears, and our hopes—and those talks would end with laughter, or sometimes with tears that turned into something softer and more forgiving.

We made love like people who knew the stakes. Not just bodies, but hearts. Each encounter was an act of trust, an affirmation that life, no matter how broken, could still bloom again.

When the lawsuits dragged on and the farm became more memory than reality, we carried that love on the road. We traveled across the Central and Eastern United States, staying in AirBNBs that became our temporary homes. Every city held a new memory, every stop a chance to be just us—free from the shadows of the past.

And yet, as healing as it was, reality has a way of creeping back in. The road was expensive, and when her daughter moved back into her life, our rhythm shifted. The love remained, but life’s circumstances pulled at the edges.
