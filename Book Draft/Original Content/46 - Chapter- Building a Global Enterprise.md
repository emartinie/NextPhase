### Chapter: Building a Global Enterprise

There are moments in my career when you know you're no longer solving technical problems—you are helping shape the future of an entire organization.

For me, one of those moments came during the global SAP ERP deployment at Gibson Brands.

This wasn't simply an IT upgrade. It was the largest technology transformation I had ever led. More than one hundred locations across North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific, Latin America, and Japan would eventually operate from a single global SAP environment. Nearly two hundred business applications would be integrated into one platform, replacing years of fragmented systems and regional workarounds.

### The Challenge

Every region had developed its own habits, procedures, and technical infrastructure.

What worked in Nashville didn't necessarily work in Amsterdam.

What worked in Japan wasn't always compatible with Latin America.

The technical problems were significant, but the real challenge wasn't technology.

It was trust.

People weren't simply being asked to learn new software.

They were being asked to abandon systems they had relied upon for years and place their confidence in something entirely new.

Technology projects succeed or fail because of people long before they fail because of computers.

### Building the Foundation

The solution required much more than installing software just like discipleship did.

We designed a global architecture using SAP, Citrix, VMware, Microsoft technologies, disaster recovery planning, encrypted communications, upgraded VPN infrastructure, and hundreds of integrated business applications working together as one ecosystem.

Behind every technical decision was a simple question:

"Will this help people do their jobs better?"

Technology was never the goal.

It was the tool.

### Leading Across Continents

One of the defining moments of the project came when I presented the deployment strategy simultaneously to hundreds of locations around the world.

Different cultures.

Different languages.

Different time zones.

One vision.

The questions were exactly what you would expect once we got into rooms with managers and power users.

"What happens if our firewall configuration fails?"

"How will legacy systems continue operating?"

"What about our distributors?"

"What happens on go-live?"

Each answer built confidence.

Every meeting became another opportunity to demonstrate that this wasn't simply an IT project.

It was organizational transformation.

### Go-Live

Months of planning eventually became one day.

Go-live.

Years of preparation would be judged in a matter of hours.

One region after another came online successfully.

The screens didn't celebrate.

There were no fireworks.

No headlines.

Just quiet confirmations that another site was operational.

For the people who had invested so much of themselves into the project, those confirmations meant everything.

### What I Learned

Looking back, the project taught me something far more important than SAP.

Leadership isn't about knowing every answer.

Leadership is helping people move confidently toward a future they cannot yet see.

Technology changes.

Software changes.

Businesses change.

People remain the center of every successful transformation.

That lesson has followed me far beyond Gibson.

It shaped how I build businesses.

It shaped how I approach leadership.

And today, it shapes how I'm building HomeGroups.
