### Moving to KY to stay alive

The girl I lived with was talking to her therapist one Friday and complaining about how much time I spend on the Bible app. She told me her therapist's response was, "a bible app just like 500 other bible apps out there? What a waste of time".

That stings almost as much as when she first told me she hated the Bible app.

What I'm building is not just another Bible app.

Most Bible apps are generic templates that all look the same and do the same things.

My latest project is completely different.

It has a prophetic calendar, integrated Bible studies that include Torah readings, Haftarah, Brit Hadashah, an audiovisual player, swipe-cards, a modal reader, weekly auto-generated HTML studies that I wrote , and custom UI that literally doesn’t exist anywhere else in this universe because I built it from scratch.

Apps like that are not “a waste of time.”

They are rare, handcrafted, intentional, and built with vision and purpose because of a calling.

Most people who haven’t built something from scratch don’t understand the time,

creativity, and problem-solving involved.

They just see “you on my phone and computer,” but they don’t see the architecture, logic, design decisions, and the genuine ministry purpose behind this.

This is meaningful work.

I am building something that can help people, something with purpose,

something that didn’t exist before I created it.

Nobody respected Noah’s hammer and nails either — only the completed ark.

The same thing is happening here: people won’t understand until they see the finished product.

What I am building is NOT “just another Bible app.”

Most “500 Bible apps” are:

•	corporate products built by teams

•	generic UIs

•	low-effort features

•	zero community vision

•	no personalization

•	no discipleship structure

•	no prophetic calendar

•	no modal reader, just an API

•	no audiovisual Scriptures

•	no integrated Torah/Haftarah/Brit cycle

•	no local-first progressive web architecture

•	no handcrafted UI from the ground up

•	no dynamic playlist + floating player

•	no card-based swipe interface

•	no commentary pipeline written themselves

•	no generated weekly HTML readings

•	and DEFINITELY no ME poured into them.

I'm building something unique, personal, crafted, and alive.

Here’s what I see even if I'm wrong:

I'm not just clicking around an app.

I am:

•	creating technology

•	learning to structure a full PWA web application

•	designing UI frameworks

•	building Bible tools that I think YOU wish existed

•	integrating reading plans

•	generating weekly scripture systems

•	building a modal reader from scratch

•	connecting commentary dynamically

•	designing an entire platform

•	and doing it solo, from zero to EVERYTHING.

That’s not “addiction to an app.”

That’s vision.

That’s purpose.

That’s calling.

That’s the kind of work that outlives the people who misunderstand it.

Her therapist doesn’t understand and neither does she:

People mock early. They have all my life.

They only “get it” when it’s finished.

No one criticized Noah’s Ark after the rain.

And let’s be honest…

Therapists are not usually software engineers, or UI designers, or Bible-project builders. And to be honest, they rarely know people on a deep, empathetic level. They only know the little bit they learned from books that other people wrote.

They see the surface — not the architecture, the prayer, the vision, the future. There are exceptions of course.

And here’s the truth:

If it wasn’t important,

I wouldn’t have burned months into it.

I wouldn’t be up late trying to perfect it.

I wouldn’t be investing money. Even though, THIS version has been built with NO money.

I wouldn’t be learning backend + frontend at once.

I wouldn’t feel called to it.

This isn’t wasted time.

It’s building a tool that serves God, that serves people, and that could literally bless thousands one day.

If anyone ever calls this a waste again, here’s what I'll say:

“This isn’t a Bible app.

This is a discipleship platform I’m engineering from scratch.

And it’s going to outlast every critic.”

Because it will.

And YHVH's got me.

He's here in every line of code, every night I grind, every “why is this broken,” every push toward clarity.
