# Chapter Six — Home by Home

When I got out of prison, I made a discovery that surprised me.

I hadn't learned about denominations from studying the Bible.

I knew denominations existed, but I had always thought of them as different church names. I assumed that churches calling themselves Bible-believing churches essentially believed the same things.

I didn't grow up in church.

I'm grateful that my mother took me to church a few times growing up. Those visits gave me some familiarity with Scripture, but I didn't know much about what most people call the Old Testament.

That's one of the reasons why, when I went to prison, I started reading the Bible at page one.

I didn't begin with a denomination's statement of faith. I began with Genesis.

## A Church on Every Street Corner

When I got out of prison, it seemed like there was a church on every street corner.

I visited several of them. When I heard something from the pulpit that didn't quite match what I had read in Scripture, I would ask to speak with the pastor.

Those conversations rarely went well.

In fact, I ended up getting kicked out of just about every church I tried.

I also discovered that some churches had what they called home groups, life groups, or other names for smaller gatherings of believers.

But what I found was that these groups were essentially branches of the churches that established them.

What I was looking for were roots growing out of Yeshua.

People talked about Him, but they didn't necessarily talk about the things He talked about.

I wanted to study those things together. I wanted Scripture to be the foundation of what we believed, rather than beginning with an established set of beliefs and then looking to Scripture to support them.

## The Picture I Saw in Scripture

One of the things that struck me in my reading of the New Testament was that the people gathered both in synagogues and in homes.

The picture I got of synagogues wasn't always a good one. Yeshua taught in them, but He also encountered opposition in them. The apostles encountered both acceptance and opposition as they went out teaching.

That didn't mean there was anything inherently wrong with gathering in a synagogue or a larger assembly.

But I saw something foundational in the gatherings that took place in people's homes.

Acts describes believers gathering in the Temple and breaking bread from house to house. Paul writes about assemblies meeting in the homes of people he knew.

The home wasn't merely a place where members of an assembly occasionally got together.

It was a place where the assembly itself existed.

I began gathering with people in what I called Bible study groups. I didn't necessarily call them Home Groups at the time.

The practice came before the name.

## Beyond the Bible Study

It seemed like common sense to me that the purpose of a Bible study group extended beyond the Bible study itself.

We were studying something that was supposed to teach us how to live.

When the Bible study was over, everyone went back out into the world. They went home to their families. They went to work. They interacted with their neighbors and with people they encountered throughout their lives.

What we studied together was supposed to help develop the people who went back out into that world.

If we studied patience, what did that mean when we went home to our children?

If we studied honesty, what did that mean when we went to work?

If we studied loving our neighbors, what did that mean when we encountered someone who needed our help?

I didn't see those things as separate from Bible study. I saw them as the reason for it.

And when we gathered together again, we had the opportunity to talk about those things, to learn from one another, and to help one another grow.

## Home by Home

I've already talked about what a home meant to me growing up and what it meant to me when I became a father.

I wanted my children to have a home that never went away.

There is something important about having a place where people know you, where you can ask questions, where you can learn, and where the people around you have an interest in your well-being.

I saw value in that same kind of relationship extending beyond my own family.

A larger gathering can bring many people together. But there is also something valuable about sitting around a table with a few people, opening Scripture together, and talking about how it relates to the lives we actually live.

I didn't see the need for one kind of gathering to eliminate the other.

What mattered to me was where we began and what we were building upon.

I wanted communities that were rooted in Scripture, where people could help one another become better husbands, wives, fathers, mothers, neighbors, and servants of our Creator.

And I wanted what happened in those homes to extend into the communities around them.

Years later, technology would give me new ways to think about connecting those communities. But the idea itself didn't require technology.

It required people.

People willing to open their homes, open their Bibles, and take an interest in the lives of one another.

We could begin with a few people.

We could begin with one home.

And we could build from there, home by home.
