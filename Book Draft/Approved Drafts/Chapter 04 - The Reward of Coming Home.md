# Chapter Four — The Reward of Coming Home

I spent approximately sixteen years working at Gibson Guitars, eventually becoming the company's Chief Information Officer.

When I think about those years, there are certainly plenty of professional accomplishments worth remembering. I had opportunities I never could have imagined when I was younger. I traveled around the world, worked with extraordinary people, and became part of a company whose name was recognized almost everywhere.

But when I think about what those years meant to me personally, my career isn't the first thing that comes to mind.

My children are.

Because throughout those years, I was trying to accomplish two things that were both extraordinarily important to me.

I wanted to be successful enough to give my family a wonderful future.

And I wanted to be present enough to enjoy that future with them.

Those two desires were not always easy to reconcile.

I understood that from the beginning.

I didn't wake up one morning, years into my career, and suddenly realize that my work was taking time away from my children. I was aware of that tension every single day of my fatherhood.

Every hour I spent at work was an hour I couldn't spend with them.

But every advancement in my career also represented another opportunity to provide for them, to give them greater security, and to build the kind of life I had always wanted my children to experience.

I was making a sacrifice for their future.

And I knew it.

What I refused to do was sacrifice their entire present in pursuit of that future.

That was the balance I spent those years trying to maintain.

## The Opportunities of a Lifetime

Working at Gibson was unlike working at most companies.

Gibson wasn't simply a manufacturer. It was a name associated with some of the greatest musicians in history, a company whose instruments had helped shape the sound of generations.

And being part of that world opened doors.

I traveled internationally, including trips to China, Japan, and Amsterdam. I met some of the most famous people in the world. I had opportunities to attend concerts, parties, and events that many people would have considered once-in-a-lifetime experiences.

There were extraordinary people to meet and extraordinary places to visit.

And there were countless opportunities to stay a little longer, socialize a little more, and enjoy the privileges that came with my position.

But I had somewhere I wanted to be.

Home.

I didn't turn down those opportunities because I failed to appreciate them. I understood perfectly well how fortunate I was to experience the world I was working in.

I simply understood something else.

No matter how famous the person standing in front of me was, there were two people waiting for me at home who mattered more.

And I wanted to get back to them.

When my responsibilities required me to travel internationally, I traveled. Obviously, I couldn't come home every night from China or Japan.

But I made those trips as short as possible.

I almost never did anything other than work while I was away. I wasn't interested in extending a business trip for sightseeing, nightlife, or entertainment.

I wanted to finish the work I had traveled there to accomplish.

And then I wanted to go home.

Not everyone around me had a family waiting for them. Some of my colleagues had different priorities and different opportunities to enjoy the experiences that came with working at Gibson.

I can't say that everyone understood my choices in exactly the same way I did.

But they respected them.

They certainly didn't look down on me for wanting to be with my children.

They understood why I chose being a good dad over being a good host.

And that was a choice I was happy to make.

## What I Was Working For

I had known instability for much of my childhood.

I knew what it was like to have your circumstances change without warning, to lose the place you called home, and to wonder what tomorrow might bring.

I wanted something different for my children.

I wanted them to have security.

I wanted them to have opportunities.

I wanted them to grow up with a freedom from worry that I hadn't always enjoyed myself.

And I was willing to work extraordinarily hard to make that possible.

Climbing the corporate ladder wasn't simply about obtaining a better title or earning a larger paycheck.

Those things had value, of course. But their greatest value to me was what they could provide for my family.

I wanted my children to grow up without having to carry the burdens I had carried.

I wanted them to have a home that never went away.

I wanted them to know that Daddy would always be trying to come home.

And I wanted their mother to have freedom, too.

I've often thought about how much of my own freedom I was willing to give up so that my family could enjoy theirs.

That was a choice I made.

But it is important to understand that their mother worked hard, too.

Her work was simply different from mine.

While I was spending my days at Gibson, she was spending hers caring for our children and managing the responsibilities that came with being home with them.

Anyone who has spent an entire day caring for young children knows that it can be exhausting. Children need constant attention. There are meals, messes, interruptions, frustrations, and a seemingly endless number of things that need to be done.

There is no reason to pretend that one kind of work is real work and another isn't.

We were both working to provide for our family, in different ways.

And I understood that she needed time for herself just as much as anyone else did.

## Coming Home

There was something I looked forward to throughout my workday.

Going home.

I don't mean that I disliked my job. Quite the opposite. Gibson was an extraordinary place to work, and I had responsibilities and opportunities that challenged me professionally.

But there was something waiting for me at home that no professional accomplishment could replace.

I got to be Daddy.

When I came home from work, their mother was free to go do whatever she wanted.

She had spent the day with the children. Now she could have some time to herself.

And I stayed home with the kids.

I never considered that a chore.

I never thought of it as another shift I had to work after finishing my day at Gibson.

It wasn't a burden I reluctantly accepted.

It was what I wanted.

I had spent the day working to build a future for my children. Coming home gave me the opportunity to enjoy being part of their lives right then and there.

And I loved that.

There is an important difference between being a father who provides for his children and being a daddy who participates in their lives.

I wanted to be both.

I didn't want to be the man whose children had everything they needed except his time.

I didn't want them to know me primarily through the things I bought or the house I provided.

I wanted them to know me because I was there.

Because we spent time together.

Because Daddy came home.

Even on the days when my responsibilities were demanding, even when I was tired, I never regarded time with my children as something I had to endure.

They were the reason I was doing everything else.

And being with them was the reward.

## The Freedom I Wanted for My Family

One of the things I valued about the life we were building was the freedom it could provide.

I wanted my wife and children to have opportunities to enjoy their days without the same pressures I was carrying at work.

I also wanted us to find opportunities to spend time together even during the workday.

I remember asking their mother to bring the children to Gibson so we could have lunch together.

Those visits happened, although they were few and far between.

I would have enjoyed more of them.

It wasn't because I wanted to turn my workplace into a family outing every day. I simply wanted more opportunities to see the people I loved.

Sometimes a lunch together would have meant an opportunity to spend time with my children that I otherwise wouldn't have had.

And those moments mattered to me.

I knew that my career required sacrifices. I had accepted that.

But I was always looking for ways to keep those sacrifices from becoming unnecessary distance between me and my family.

I wanted to build a successful career without allowing that career to become the center of my life.

Because it wasn't.

My family was.

## Success Meant Something Different to Me

There are many ways people measure professional success.

Some measure it by their title.

Others measure it by their salary, their influence, the people they know, or the places their careers take them.

I understood all those measurements. I had worked hard to achieve things that mattered professionally, and I was proud of what I had accomplished.

But fatherhood had changed the meaning of success for me.

A promotion was valuable because it could help me provide a better life for my family.

A business trip was worthwhile because it served a purpose.

A demanding responsibility was worth accepting when it helped me build the future I wanted for my children.

But none of those things could replace the time I spent with them.

And I knew that while I was living it.

I wasn't waiting until retirement to discover what mattered.

I wasn't planning to make up for my children's childhoods after they were grown.

I wanted to be there while they were growing up.

I wanted them to know that their daddy worked hard.

But I also wanted them to know that his work wasn't more important than they were.

Sometimes that meant declining invitations.

Sometimes it meant getting on a plane and coming home instead of staying another day.

Sometimes it meant leaving behind an opportunity to spend time with people the rest of the world might have been excited to meet.

And sometimes it meant walking through my own front door after a long day, knowing that my evening would be spent with my children.

Of all those choices, the last one was often the easiest.

## The Life I Was Building

Looking back at my years at Gibson, I can see how much of my life revolved around trying to honor two commitments.

I wanted to be the best professional I could be.

And I wanted to be the best daddy I could be.

Those commitments sometimes competed for my time, but they weren't enemies.

My work helped me provide for my family.

My family gave my work its deepest purpose.

I didn't always have as much free time as I wanted. I certainly made sacrifices. And there were opportunities I passed up that I will probably never have again.

But I don't look at those missed concerts, parties, or evenings with famous people as the great losses of my life.

I knew what I was choosing.

I was choosing to be with my children.

I was choosing the life I had worked so hard to give them.

I was choosing to come home.

When I was young, I could scarcely have imagined becoming the Chief Information Officer of Gibson Guitars. I couldn't have imagined traveling around the world or meeting some of the people I would eventually meet.

Those were extraordinary experiences.

But when I think about the man I was trying to become, the title that mattered most wasn't Chief Information Officer.

It was Daddy.

And the greatest reward of my career wasn't something I received at Gibson.

It was waiting for me when I got home.
