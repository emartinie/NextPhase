# Chapter Nine — Big Whiskers Farm: From Vision to Reality

For years, I had been arranging my life around two very important responsibilities.

I had a career that enabled me to provide for my family, and I had a family that gave me a reason to have that career.

But there was something my career couldn't give me more of.

Time with my children.

## An Exit Strategy

I have already explained how important it was to me to come home at the end of every day. As much as I enjoyed many aspects of my work at Gibson, the greatest reward of that work was what it enabled me to provide for my family.

Eventually, my wife and I began working toward an exit strategy from Gibson.

I wasn't looking for a way to avoid working. I was looking for a way to spend more of my life with my children.

I had already begun exploring what we could accomplish on the property where we lived. I was learning about aquaponics and developing plans for making our homestead more productive. Much of what I had been researching and teaching about sustainable living was finding its way into my own backyard.

As my wife and I considered our future and I began looking at properties, she sent me a link to a place that was for sale.

It was called Big Whiskers Farm.

I want to give her credit for that because it was she who brought that particular property into my life.

What I saw in Big Whiskers was the possibility of bringing together a great many things that had previously occupied different parts of my life.

There was the work I had been doing to provide for my children. There was my interest in sustainable living. There were the communities I had been developing through technology, the things I had been teaching, and the things I had been learning from other people.

Here was a place where some of those things might actually come together.

And I might be able to do that work without having to leave my family every morning to do it.

## The Long Road to Big Whiskers

Seeing the property and acquiring it were two entirely different things.

The process of trying to purchase Big Whiskers stretched on for nearly three years. There were negotiations, setbacks, and conversations that seemed to bring us closer to an agreement only to leave the future of the property uncertain again.

The owner could never quite seem to decide whether he wanted to sell.

But the possibilities of the place continued to interest me.

I wasn't simply looking at acreage or considering what the buildings might be worth. I was considering what we could do there.

What could we grow?

What could we teach?

What could my children learn by growing up in an environment like that?

What could other families learn by spending time there?

These weren't entirely new questions for me. They were extensions of questions I had already been asking in the context of my own home and the communities I had been developing.

The scale of the opportunity was different.

The foundation was not.

## Melissa

There was another woman who would play an extraordinary role in making the purchase of Big Whiskers possible.

Her name was Melissa.

I had physically met Melissa years earlier through one of the emergency preparedness groups in which I taught. By the time my wife and I were working toward our exit strategy from Gibson, Melissa had become one of the members I most valued in one of my most important Facebook groups.

We had developed relationships in that group that meant considerably more to me than the number of people who followed our discussions.

We studied Scripture. We discussed the things we were learning and the things we believed. We challenged one another and talked about how those things might find expression in the lives we lived.

And somewhere in the course of those conversations, the people in that community had become familiar with what I was trying to accomplish.

One day, quite unexpectedly, Melissa sent me a message.

She told me that she had been praying and that our Creator had put it on her heart to buy the farm for me.

I didn't exactly know what that meant.

But before I even began trying to understand what it might mean financially, there was something much more important that I wanted us to understand.

Was this really what our Creator was leading us to do?

## Seeking Confirmation

My response to Melissa was to ask her to continue praying.

It wasn't enough for me to know that she believed God had put something on her heart. I wanted her to go back to Him and seek confirmation.

And I needed to do the same.

I told my wife what Melissa had said, and I asked her to go to our Creator for confirmation as well.

There were three of us involved in this, and I wanted all three of us to seek His direction.

I believed there was a great deal of responsibility involved in receiving something of that magnitude from another person.

I also understood that wanting something to happen was not the same thing as knowing that it was supposed to happen.

I had spent years trying to acquire this property. Naturally, I wanted to see the purchase become possible. But the fact that an opportunity appeared to present itself didn't mean that I should simply assume that my desire and the will of our Creator were the same thing.

That was why prayer was so important to me.

Eventually, Melissa came back to me and told me that she had continued praying and still believed that this was what she was supposed to do.

I still didn't quite understand what she was proposing, so I suggested that she make a proposal.

What ultimately came out of that was a quarter of a million dollars toward making the purchase of Big Whiskers Farm possible.

Think about where that relationship began.

It began years earlier in a community where I was teaching people about emergency preparedness.

It continued through a community where we studied Scripture and discussed the things that mattered to us.

And now, through that relationship, someone was offering to help my family in a way that I could never have anticipated when we first met.

I will always be grateful for what Melissa did to make the acquisition of that farm possible.

And I will always be grateful to my wife for bringing the property to my attention and for being part of the effort to develop that future for our family.

Both women belong in the story of how Big Whiskers came into my life.

## Five Checks

Even with the financial opportunity before us, there was still the matter of actually acquiring the property.

After years of negotiations, I eventually made a very straightforward offer.

As I remember putting it:

> How about I give you five $50,000 checks, and you give me the farm?

Less than a week later, the papers were signed.

After nearly three years of trying to purchase the property, Big Whiskers Farm was finally mine.

There is a considerable difference between developing a vision for something and finding yourself in a position to begin doing it.

For years, I had been making resources available to people through websites and Facebook groups. I had been teaching people about sustainable living and discussing the ways in which believers could help one another.

I had been trying to develop some of those same capabilities in my own home.

Now I had a place where I could begin exploring those possibilities on a much larger scale.

## A Different Kind of Work

My years at Gibson had taught me a great deal about technology, leadership, and the practical difficulties of bringing people and resources together.

I didn't suddenly stop being interested in those things because I had acquired a farm.

In many ways, the farm gave me a different environment in which to apply them.

I was interested in hydroponics, aquaponics, aquaculture, and controlled-environment agriculture. I wanted to explore ways of growing food that didn't necessarily depend upon the conventional methods with which most people were familiar.

I was interested in emergency communications and in teaching people how to develop practical skills that could benefit their households.

And I was interested in what could happen when people came together in a place where they could actually see and participate in some of the things we had been discussing.

Technology had enabled me to connect with people around the world.

The farm gave me a place where people could gather.

I didn't see those as competing endeavors.

There were things we could accomplish through technology that we couldn't accomplish simply by gathering on a piece of property in Tennessee.

And there were things we could accomplish by gathering together that we couldn't accomplish simply by sitting in front of computers.

I wanted both.

## More Than a Place to Fish

Big Whiskers was already a place where people could come to fish.

That was a wonderful opportunity in itself.

Families could spend time together. Children could experience the excitement of catching fish. Older people could enjoy being outside and spending time around the water.

Fishing tournaments brought people together, and the farm welcomed people with different abilities and from different walks of life.

Some of the things I wanted to accomplish at Big Whiskers involved sophisticated technology and considerable research.

But some of the most meaningful things that happened there didn't require sophisticated technology at all.

They involved people spending time with one another.

A child catching a fish.

A family enjoying a day together.

A conversation that might never have taken place if two people hadn't happened to be standing beside the same pond.

Those things mattered to me.

I didn't want the value of Big Whiskers to be measured exclusively by how much it could produce, whether that meant agricultural production or financial revenue.

I also wanted to consider what it could provide for the people who came there.

Opportunities to learn.

Opportunities to develop relationships.

Opportunities for families to spend time together.

And those were precisely the kinds of opportunities I had wanted more of in my own life.

## From Vision to Reality

When I think about how Big Whiskers came into my life, I can't separate it from the relationships that preceded it.

My children were the reason I wanted to change the way I lived and worked.

My wife introduced me to the property that would become such an important part of our lives.

Melissa, someone I had come to know through the communities in which I taught, helped make its acquisition possible.

And the knowledge I had accumulated over the years, along with the things other people had taught me, gave me a foundation upon which to begin developing it.

There is a passage in Scripture that I think is particularly relevant to this part of my life.

In 1 Corinthians 12, Paul describes the body as having many members. He explains that the eye cannot say to the hand that it has no need of it, nor can the head say that to the feet.

Each has something to contribute.

Each also has need of the others.

I had spent a great deal of my life thinking about what I could do for other people. But my own life was also being shaped by the things other people were doing for me.

That is an important part of what I want HomeGroups to become.

Not a community built around one person who has all the answers or possesses all the resources, but a community in which people recognize what they have to offer one another.

Big Whiskers gave me an opportunity to begin living out more of that vision.

Acquiring the farm was an extraordinary experience.

But the acquisition itself wasn't the objective.

It was what we could do with it.

And, perhaps more importantly, what we could do together.
