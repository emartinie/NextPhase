### Chapter 20 – The Cost of a Vision

Every meaningful vision has a price.

The larger the vision, the less likely that price can be measured in dollars alone.

I learned that lesson at Big Whiskers Farm.

From the outside, people saw ponds full of fish, families enjoying tournaments, and a peaceful country setting.

They saw the finished product.

Very few saw the years of sacrifice that made it possible.

### Three Years to Say "Yes"

It took nearly three years before the farm finally became mine.

During that time I walked the property countless times, learning every hill, every pond, and every possibility it contained.

The negotiations were unlike anything I had experienced.

Reality television producers.

Changing ownership expectations.

Competing interests.

Promises.

Disappointments.

At one point I finally stopped negotiating around the edges and simply made an offer.

"How about I give you five $50,000 checks, and you give me the farm?"

Sometimes clarity succeeds where complexity fails.

Within days, the deal was moving forward.

### Building the Dream

Owning the farm was only the beginning.

The barn needed work.

Water systems had to be upgraded.

Security installed.

Roads improved.

Equipment purchased.

Buildings repaired.

Every improvement represented another investment in a future that still existed mostly in my imagination.

People often assume entrepreneurs spend their days dreaming.

In reality, they spend much of their time solving problems no one else even knows exist.

### The Weight of Leadership

As the vision expanded, something else became increasingly clear.

The vision belonged to me.

Others helped.

Friends volunteered.

Partners joined for seasons.

Family supported the effort in different ways.

But when difficult decisions had to be made...

When payroll couldn't be met...

When another unexpected expense appeared...

When another partner walked away...

The responsibility remained mine.

Leadership can be lonely.

Not because people don't care.

Because no one else carries the vision in exactly the same way.

### Learning to Delegate

That season also forced me to recognize my own limitations.

There were too many responsibilities for one person.

Too many ideas.

Too many projects.

Too many decisions.

I realized that building something significant requires more than hard work.

It requires trust.

It requires capable people who believe in the mission enough to make it their own.

One lesson became unforgettable:

Do what you do best.

Delegate the rest.

I didn't always succeed at that.

But I never stopped learning.

### Reflection

Looking back now, I understand that the greatest cost wasn't financial.

It was emotional.

Every dream carries the possibility of disappointment.

Every vision risks failure.

Yet I would rather spend my life pursuing something meaningful than protecting myself from disappointment.

Because even when visions change...

The person they shape remains.
