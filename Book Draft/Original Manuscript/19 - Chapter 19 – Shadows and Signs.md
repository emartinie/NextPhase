### Chapter 19 – Shadows and Signs

There are seasons in life when events seem almost impossible to explain.

Some people dismiss them as coincidence.

Others immediately call them miracles.

I've learned to be cautious with both conclusions.

But I also believe there are moments when life leaves an impression so deep that you never quite see the world the same way again.

### A Mountain Cabin

After Wendy left with the children, I wasn't sure when I would see them again.

Then, unexpectedly, I was invited to spend time with them at a cabin in the mountains.

There was one condition:

No beer.

No cigarettes.

That wasn't much of a sacrifice.

Seeing my children again felt like breathing after holding my breath for far too long.

For a little while, everything almost felt normal.

We laughed.

We spent time together.

For a brief moment, life seemed familiar again.

### The Quiet Hours

Late that night, after everyone else had gone to sleep, I found myself alone with my thoughts.

Pain has a way of making the quiet louder.

Questions I'd carried for months resurfaced.

What happens now?

How do I rebuild?

How do I become the father my children still need?

Those weren't questions anyone else could answer for me.

### Learning to Listen

One lesson that season taught me was the importance of stillness.

In the busiest years of my career, I solved problems by working harder.

This season demanded something different.

Listening.

Not merely listening to other people.

Listening to God.

Listening to my own conscience.

Listening long enough for fear to stop talking.

Whether through prayer, Scripture, or simple silence, I began discovering that clarity rarely arrives during chaos.

It often comes afterward.

### Strength Through Weakness

I had spent much of my adult life believing strength meant having answers.

That season taught me something different.

Strength sometimes means admitting you don't know.

It means taking the next faithful step without seeing the entire road ahead.

It means choosing hope when circumstances offer very little reason for optimism.

Looking back, I don't remember every conversation.

I don't remember every detail.

What I remember is the growing conviction that my life wasn't over.

God wasn't finished writing my story.

### Reflection

Some chapters change us because of what happened.

Others change us because of what happened inside us.

The mountain cabin became one of those places I still return to in memory.

Not because it solved every problem.

Because it reminded me that even in seasons of uncertainty, God often meets us in the quiet places before He changes the visible ones.
