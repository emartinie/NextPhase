### Chapter 11 – Prophecy in Motion

There came a point in my study when prophecy stopped feeling like a collection of mysterious predictions.

Instead, I began to see it as the continuation of the same covenant story that runs from Genesis to Revelation.

The more I studied, the less interested I became in sensational headlines and speculative timelines.

I wanted to understand how the prophets understood God's relationship with His people.

### Returning to the Covenants

For years I had read the Bible one passage at a time.

Eventually I stepped back and began looking at it as one unfolding narrative.

Every covenant.

Every promise.

Every warning.

Every act of redemption.

They all revealed the same God faithfully working through history.

That realization changed how I approached prophecy.

Instead of asking, "What secret code am I supposed to discover?" I began asking, "What has God consistently been saying from the beginning?"

### Seeing New Connections

As I continued studying Scripture, I started noticing connections I had never seen before.

Passover seemed to illuminate the work of Messiah.

The prophets echoed themes established centuries earlier in the Torah.

Jeremiah's promise of a New Covenant pointed back to promises that had never been abandoned.

Rather than viewing Scripture as divided into unrelated eras, I began seeing remarkable continuity throughout the entire biblical story.

Those discoveries didn't end my study.

They fueled it.

### Sharing the Journey

When these ideas became part of our HomeGroups conversations, people responded in different ways.

Some were excited.

Others were skeptical.

Many simply appreciated having a place where questions were welcome.

That was always my goal.

Not to pressure people into agreeing with me.

To invite them to study the Scriptures for themselves.

If our discussions encouraged someone to open the Bible with fresh curiosity, they had already been worthwhile.

### Holding Convictions with Humility

The deeper I studied prophecy, the more convinced I became that humility is essential.

History is filled with sincere people who confidently predicted events that never happened.

I didn't want to repeat that mistake.

I wanted to remain teachable.

To keep learning.

To allow Scripture—not speculation—to shape my understanding.

### Reflection

Prophecy has become, for me, less about predicting tomorrow and more about recognizing the faithfulness of God across generations.

Every fulfilled promise strengthens confidence in the One who made it.

Every unanswered question reminds me there is still more to learn.

The journey continues.

Not driven by fear.

But by hope.
