### HomeGroups Vision 2026

### Twenty Years Later

#### Introduction

When I first began thinking about HomeGroups, my vision was to help revive discipleship through relationships, technology, and local communities. Over the years, the methods have changed, the technology has changed, and even many of my assumptions have changed.

The mission has not.

If anything, time has simplified it.

Today I no longer see HomeGroups primarily as a ministry, a website, an application, or an organization. Those are merely tools. HomeGroups is an attempt to remove barriers between people and truth while helping believers become mature disciples who can disciple others.

The greatest lesson I have learned is that technology is never the destination. Technology is simply another language through which people can teach, encourage, investigate, and serve.

### The Mission

My mission is simple.

Study the entire Bible together.

Test everything.

Love one another.

Equip disciples who make disciples.

Everything else exists to support those four objectives.

### The Foundation

I believe truth has nothing to fear from investigation.

Every tradition...

Every doctrine...

Every interpretation...

Every historical claim...

Every scientific claim...

Every archaeological discovery...

Every translation...

Every commentary...

Every one of them should be willing to stand in the light.

The purpose of HomeGroups is not to defend traditions.

The purpose is to investigate truth.

Where evidence changes our understanding, we should change.

Truth remains true whether we discover it or not.

### Technology

Technology has become one of the greatest gifts ever given to humanity.

It can also become one of its greatest distractions.

Our goal is never to replace pastors.

Never replace churches.

Never replace families.

Never replace friendships.

Never replace the Holy Spirit.

Technology should remove friction.

It should make learning easier.

Relationships stronger.

Questions easier to ask.

Scripture easier to explore.

Leaders easier to equip.

Communities easier to build.

If technology ever becomes the center of HomeGroups, we have failed.

People are the mission.

### Discipleship

Discipleship is not attendance.

It is not information.

It is not agreement.

It is transformation.

The purpose of study is not merely knowing more.

The purpose of study is becoming more faithful.

Knowledge without obedience produces pride.

Knowledge combined with humility produces wisdom.

### Community

Healthy communities are built on relationships, not institutions.

Followers become students.

Students become servants.

Servants become leaders.

Leaders equip other leaders.

Leadership is multiplication.

Not control.

No single person owns the Kingdom of God.

Every believer has something to contribute.

### Investigation

Questions are not enemies of faith.

Questions are invitations to learn.

HomeGroups exists to investigate Scripture with intellectual honesty.

We welcome difficult passages.

Historical challenges.

Archaeology.

Science.

Languages.

Jewish history.

Early Christianity.

Church history.

Textual criticism.

Ancient literature.

Every discipline has something to contribute.

The goal is not certainty about everything.

The goal is increasing confidence where evidence is strong while remaining humble where evidence is incomplete.

### Scripture

The Bible remains the center.

Not because it answers every modern question.

But because it reveals God's interaction with humanity and ultimately points us toward Messiah.

Every tool in HomeGroups exists to help people understand Scripture more clearly.

Maps.

Languages.

Interlinear studies.

Historical timelines.

Audio.

Commentaries.

Cross references.

Intertext.

Commandments.

Articles.

Research papers.

None of these replace Scripture.

They illuminate it.

### AI

Artificial Intelligence is neither savior nor enemy.

Like every technology before it, it reflects the intentions of those who use it.

Used wisely, AI can organize information.

Translate languages.

Summarize research.

Connect ideas.

Generate learning materials.

Ask difficult questions.

Reveal patterns.

It should never replace discernment.

The Holy Spirit remains our teacher.

AI is simply another tool sitting on the workbench.

### Leadership

Leadership is measured less by followers than by leaders produced.

Our goal is not to create dependence.

It is to create confidence.

Every believer should eventually become capable of helping someone else understand Scripture more clearly than they did yesterday.

### HomeGroups

HomeGroups is no longer simply a website.

Or an app.

Or a curriculum.

It is becoming a research platform.

A discipleship platform.

A learning platform.

A publishing platform.

A collaboration platform.

A historical archive.

A place where questions are welcomed and evidence is preserved.

### Success

Success is not measured by downloads.

Or page views.

Or attendance.

Or revenue.

Or popularity.

Success is measured one life at a time.

One family at a time.

One home group at a time.

One disciple becoming another disciple-maker.

### Looking Forward

Twenty years ago I thought I needed to build an organization.

Today I believe I need to build tools.

Not tools that replace people.

Tools that empower people.

The Kingdom of God has always advanced through ordinary people faithfully using whatever God placed into their hands.

In previous generations those tools were scrolls, letters, printing presses, schools, and radio.

Today they include the internet, mobile devices, open data, and artificial intelligence.

Tomorrow they will include technologies we cannot yet imagine.

The tools will change.

The mission will not.

Study the Scriptures.

Test everything.

Love one another.

Help the next person follow Messiah a little more faithfully than they could yesterday.

If HomeGroups accomplishes that—even for a small number of people—it will have fulfilled its purpose.

### January 2021 – A Defining Turning Point

In January 2021, my life was permanently altered by a catastrophic automobile accident. The physical injuries alone were severe, but the accident exposed something even more significant: how quickly every assumption about life, health, work, and the future can disappear. The event left me with a concussion, substantial orthopedic injuries, and little personal memory of the hours immediately following the crash. Much of what occurred had to be reconstructed later through the observations of others, medical records, photographs, and official documentation.

The experience fundamentally changed the way I think about truth and documentation. Memory is imperfect. Even sincere people remember traumatic events differently. Accurate history often requires gathering independent witnesses, comparing evidence, and allowing the facts to emerge from multiple perspectives rather than relying upon a single narrative. That lesson would later influence both my investigative writing and the design philosophy behind HomeGroups.

The accident also reshaped my understanding of leadership. During recovery I experienced firsthand how dependent we become upon family, medical professionals, friends, and complete strangers during moments of crisis. Leadership is not merely directing others; it is creating systems, documentation, and tools that allow people to help one another effectively when life becomes difficult or unpredictable.

Recovery required patience, humility, and a willingness to rebuild. Projects that once seemed urgent became secondary to healing, reflection, and reevaluating priorities. Many of the ideas that would later emerge in the HomeGroups project—including better documentation, repeatable systems, long-term knowledge preservation, simplified discipleship, and technology designed to empower rather than complicate—were strengthened by this season of recovery.

Looking back, I no longer view the accident simply as a personal tragedy. It became one of several defining inflection points that redirected both my personal life and my life's work. Rather than ending a vision, it clarified it. The experience reinforced the conviction that our time is limited, that truth deserves careful investigation, that knowledge should be preserved for those who come after us, and that the most enduring work we leave behind is the work that equips others to continue long after we are gone.
