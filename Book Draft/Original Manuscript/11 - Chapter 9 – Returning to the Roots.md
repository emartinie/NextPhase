### Chapter 9 – Returning to the Roots

As the conversations around the farm continued, I found myself returning again and again to one question:

What did the first believers actually believe?

Not what later traditions taught.

Not what denominations argued.

What did the Scriptures themselves say?

That question sent me back to the beginning.

### A Covenant Story

For years I had approached the Bible as a collection of individual books.

Gradually I began to see something different.

It wasn't sixty-six disconnected writings.

It was one unfolding covenant story.

Every promise.

Every commandment.

Every prophet.

Every act of redemption.

They all pointed to the same faithful God.

The more I studied, the more convinced I became that God's character is revealed through His faithfulness to His covenants.

He does not abandon what He establishes.

He fulfills it.

### Reading the Bible as One Story

This changed the way I read Scripture.

Instead of separating the "Old Testament" from the "New Testament," I began asking how they fit together.

Questions replaced assumptions.

How did Yeshua understand the Torah?

How did the apostles read the prophets?

How would the earliest disciples have understood covenant, Sabbath, Passover, and the appointed times?

Those questions led me into historical study, Jewish context, and the Hebrew foundations of the faith.

I wasn't looking for something new.

I was looking for something older.

### Challenging My Own Assumptions

Some of what I discovered confirmed beliefs I already held.

Some of it challenged them.

That was uncomfortable.

But I've learned that growth often begins where comfort ends.

I made a commitment to follow the evidence wherever it led, even when it required me to reconsider long-held conclusions.

That wasn't an act of rebellion.

It was an act of honesty.

### Conversations Around the Fire

These discoveries naturally became part of the conversations at the farm.

We talked about covenant.

The feasts.

The Sabbath.

The prophets.

The life and ministry of Yeshua.

People came from many different backgrounds, bringing different experiences and different conclusions.

I wasn't interested in winning debates.

I wanted people to become students of Scripture.

If our conversations encouraged someone to open the Bible with fresh eyes, they had accomplished their purpose.

### Looking Forward

I still don't claim to have every answer.

The deeper I study, the more I realize how much remains to be learned.

But I have become convinced of one thing:

Scripture rewards those who approach it with humility.

Not with the determination to defend what they already believe—

but with the willingness to discover what God has already said.
