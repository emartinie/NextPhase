### Chapter 6 – The Farm

There comes a point in life when success is no longer measured by the size of the office or the title on the business card.

For me, that point was a farm called Big Whiskers.

After years in the corporate world, I traded conference rooms for ponds, servers for tractors, and strategic planning meetings for early mornings spent walking the property. It wasn't an escape from work—it was a different kind of work.

I wasn't just buying land.

I was buying a vision.

### A Dream Worth Chasing

Acquiring the farm didn't happen overnight.

The process took years of negotiations, setbacks, and perseverance.

When the opportunity finally came together, I remember making an offer that came straight from instinct:

"How about I give you five $50,000 checks, and you give me the farm?"

Less than a week later, the papers were signed.

The farm was mine.

### Building Something Bigger

From the beginning, I wanted Big Whiskers to become more than a place to fish.

Families gathered around the ponds.

Children caught fish they would remember for the rest of their lives.

Fishing tournaments brought people together.

I taught preparedness, experimented with aquaponics, explored controlled-environment agriculture, and looked for new ways to combine education with community.

The farm wasn't just property.

It was becoming a place where people could learn, build relationships, and find hope.

### When Everything Fell Apart

Dreams are rarely tested while everything is going well.

Mine was tested when nearly every source of support disappeared.

Partners left.

Friends disappeared.

The marriage unraveled.

Financial realities surfaced that I hadn't fully understood, including debts and liens that had been hidden from me. Suddenly, the dream I had spent years building began slipping through my hands.

For a while, it felt as though I was trying to hold an entire world together by myself.

### Rebuilding

When everything around you collapses, you discover what cannot be taken away.

Character.

Faith.

Purpose.

Those became the foundation for rebuilding.

I cleaned.

Repaired.

Restocked ponds.

Fixed fences.

Started over.

Not because it was easy, but because I believed the vision was still worth pursuing.

Each small step became a declaration that failure would not have the final word.

### Hope Returns

As time passed, something remarkable happened.

People began coming back.

Families returned to the ponds.

Conversations started around campfires.

Questions about life, faith, and purpose naturally found their way into everyday moments.

The farm slowly became what I had hoped it would be from the beginning—not merely a business, but a place where restoration could happen.

I realized that what people were often searching for wasn't simply recreation.

They were searching for hope.

### Looking Back

The corporate years taught me how to build organizations.

The farm taught me how to build people.

One taught me strategy.

The other taught me endurance.

Both taught me that leadership is measured less by what you accomplish and more by what remains standing after the storms have passed.

Today I see the farm not as the end of one chapter, but as the beginning of another.

Sometimes God builds our greatest work only after everything else has been stripped away.
