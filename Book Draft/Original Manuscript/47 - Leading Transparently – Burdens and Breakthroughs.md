### Leading Transparently – Burdens and Breakthroughs

There came a point at Big Whiskers when I felt like the last man standing. The farm was supposed to be a place of abundance—people fishing, kids laughing, tournaments bringing the community together—but behind the scenes, I was drowning in responsibilities that no one else seemed willing to carry.

I wrote down exactly what I was facing:

•	I needed a cashier—Wendy and the kids only came when they felt like it, which was almost never.

•	I needed a hemp grower—Jay, Wendy, Jerry, all had abandoned me.

•	I needed a groundskeeper—my cousin had walked away.

•	I needed a baitfish and aquaponics manager, an events and media manager, a property and office manager, a pond manager, and someone to run tournaments.

•	What I had was me. Alone.

I was getting bogged down. Too many boards, too many projects, lists, tasks, roadblocks, milestones. I needed employees, but we couldn’t afford them yet. Friends, partners, even family had drifted away.

And so I learned something crucial: it’s harder to work with immediate family and friends than it is to just hire employees. That realization cut deep, but it clarified my leadership. I started telling myself:

“Do what you do best, and try to delegate the rest.”

I sat down and forced myself to create structure. Lists of tasks. Schedules. I asked the tough questions—What are the most important things I need to attend to in the morning? What will make me better tonight than I was when I woke up?

These were not just tasks; they were a lifeline. I began navigating by my higher values, by the vision that had brought me here in the first place. I decided that no matter how many abandoned me, the dream would not be abandoned.

Upgrades and Improvements – The Hidden Cost of Vision

When people came to Big Whiskers, they saw a charming farm with tournaments, families fishing, and laughter echoing across the ponds. What they didn’t see was the price of building that dream—not just in dollars, but in sweat, sacrifice, and sleepless nights.

The initial upgrades were just the beginning:

•	Barn – $20K

•	Well water conversion – $4K

•	Fencing – $1K

•	Security Cameras – $1K

•	Dirt Work & New Grass Seed – $10K

•	Kitchen Appliances & Bedroom – $2K

These numbers only scratched the surface. After pouring in a six-figure investment, we poured in another six figures, and then more. Each improvement felt like throwing another log on a fire that wouldn’t stop burning.

But this is what people don’t talk about when they romanticize entrepreneurship:

•	No one else carries the financial weight.

•	No one else lies awake at 3 a.m. wondering how to stretch the next dollar.

•	No one else feels the risk in their bones.

Employees, workers, even volunteers—they show up for their role, they collect what’s theirs, and they go home. Even if their hearts are in the right place, they don’t carry the vision.

Even my own family, my wife and kids, didn’t feel the burden the way I did. They loved the farm, but they didn’t bleed for it.

That’s the lonely truth of leadership—the vision belongs to you, and so does the cost.
