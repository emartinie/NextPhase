### Chapter 13 – The Long Road to Big Whiskers Farm

Some dreams come together in a weekend.

Others take years.

Big Whiskers Farm belonged to the second category.

From the moment I first saw the property, I knew it was unlike anything else I had ever considered buying. It wasn't simply land with a few buildings on it. It represented the possibility of creating a place where faith, education, innovation, and community could exist side by side.

The problem was that almost everyone else considered it impossible to buy.

### Three Years of Persistence

The process stretched on for nearly three years.

There were negotiations.

False starts.

Disappointments.

Conversations that seemed promising one week and hopeless the next.

At times the property felt more like a television production than a real estate transaction. There were competing interests, changing priorities, and plenty of reasons to believe the deal would never happen.

Yet every setback only strengthened my conviction that this was where I was supposed to be.

### The Conversation That Changed Everything

Eventually the negotiations reached a turning point.

Instead of trying to outmaneuver everyone involved, I chose a different approach.

I spoke plainly.

I wasn't interested in disrupting anyone's plans.

I simply wanted the opportunity to purchase the property and move forward.

Sometimes honesty accomplishes what strategy cannot.

That conversation became the turning point.

The impossible slowly became possible.

### Building More Than a Farm

Owning the farm wasn't the finish line.

It was the beginning of another vision.

We experimented with hydroponics.

Aquaponics.

Aquaculture.

Indoor growing environments.

Preparedness training.

Amateur radio.

Fishing events.

Educational workshops.

Every project pointed toward the same larger purpose.

The farm wasn't becoming a collection of attractions.

It was becoming a place where people could learn practical skills, build relationships, and discover hope.

Technology and agriculture weren't competing ideas.

They complemented one another.

Innovation served people.

People became the mission.

### A Place for Everyone

One of my favorite memories wasn't tied to a business milestone.

It was watching families gather.

Children catching fish.

Grandparents sitting quietly beside the water.

Friends sharing meals and conversations that lasted long after sunset.

The farm had become something I could never have manufactured through planning alone.

People felt at home there.

They belonged.

That mattered more to me than any financial success the property could have produced.

### Looking Back

When I think about Big Whiskers today, I don't immediately remember contracts or negotiations.

I remember people.

The laughter.

The conversations.

The lessons learned.

The quiet moments that reminded me why I had pursued the property in the first place.

The farm taught me that perseverance isn't simply refusing to quit.

It's remaining faithful to a vision long enough to watch it become reality.
