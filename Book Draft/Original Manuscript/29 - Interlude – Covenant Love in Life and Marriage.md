### Interlude – Covenant Love in Life and Marriage

The story of the covenants is not just theology to me—it is life. A covenant is not a contract where you protect your own interests; it is a vow where you give yourself away. It is the way God has always loved His people, and it is the only model that makes sense for marriage, friendship, and even the partnerships we form in this world.

When I reflect on my own journey, I see where covenant was honored—and where it was broken. I see the beauty of faithfulness, and I feel the sting of betrayal. I know what it is to stand alone when promises fail, and I know what it is to feel the healing power of being truly loved.

When my first marriage crumbled under the weight of anger, silence, and secrets, I carried wounds I thought would never heal. Then came a season of unexpected grace—a time when love returned to me, not as something I earned but as something I was given. With Amy, I rediscovered what covenant love looks like: intimacy without fear, partnership without conditions, passion without shame.

Our time together at the farm was sacred. We didn’t just share a bed; we shared dreams, prayers, and the belief that love can restore what life has tried to destroy. It was healing. It was fire. It was the kind of love that makes you believe in soulmates again—the kind that doesn’t just fill a void but calls you higher.

This is why the Bible’s language of covenant resonates so deeply with me. Love is not a feeling that fades; it is a vow that endures. When God says, “I will betroth you to me forever” (Hosea 2:19), He is speaking about a love that no failure can erase and no distance can break. It is the kind of love that rebuilds farms after they’ve been stripped, hearts after they’ve been shattered, and lives after they’ve been burned down to the foundation.

The covenant is the anchor.

The love is the flame.

And both have carried me through.
