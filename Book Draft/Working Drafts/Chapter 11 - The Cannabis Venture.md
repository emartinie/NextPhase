# Chapter Eleven — The Cannabis Venture

One of the reasons I wanted to leave Gibson was to spend more of my life with my children.

But leaving Gibson didn't mean leaving behind my responsibility to provide for them.

I was still a father. I was still a businessman. And I was looking at the opportunities available to me as the owner of Big Whiskers Farm.

One of those opportunities involved a plant that had already played a rather extraordinary role in the story of my life.

Cannabis.

## Questioning What We Inherit

In Jeremiah 16:19, the nations acknowledge that they have inherited lies from their fathers. In Ezekiel 34, our Creator speaks against the shepherds of Israel who feed themselves rather than taking care of their sheep.

Those passages have considerable significance to me.

Through my years of studying Scripture, I had learned how important it was to examine the things we had been taught rather than simply accepting them because they had been repeated for generations.

I don't think that responsibility ends when we close our Bibles.

It applies to the things we are taught about our own country, its history, and the decisions that have shaped the society in which we live.

My study of the history of cannabis had led me to question much of the narrative Americans had inherited concerning that plant.

I could devote a great deal of this book to explaining why. But I think it is important here to establish a few distinctions.

I had not been a consumer of cannabis since high school. It was not part of my personal life, and my interest in the plant as an entrepreneur did not arise from a desire to consume it.

Nevertheless, I recognized that compounds found in cannabis could have therapeutic value.

I have often made the point that a person's reason for consuming a substance does not determine all of its effects. Someone who uses cannabis recreationally does not, by virtue of that intention, eliminate whatever therapeutic effects its compounds might have. Nor does using something for therapeutic reasons eliminate its potential risks.

I could recognize potential benefits without being a consumer myself.

And I could question the history of cannabis prohibition without making cannabis consumption part of my life.

## A New Agricultural Opportunity

I had been fully supportive of President Trump when he first took office.

One of the decisions made during his first term that I particularly appreciated was the signing of the 2018 Farm Bill on December 20 of that year.

The legislation removed hemp, as defined by the law, from federal controlled-substance restrictions and established a framework for regulated commercial production.

At the time, the federal definition distinguished hemp by a delta-9 THC concentration of no more than 0.3 percent of its dry weight.

Hemp is cannabis.

I think it is important to say that plainly because I don't want to create a false distinction between two entirely different plants in order to make the story of my life more comfortable.

There was, however, an important legal distinction between hemp and marijuana.

What the Farm Bill gave me, as it gave other entrepreneurs across the country, was an idea.

I believed the decision to legalize hemp was the right one. I still do.

And as someone who had already invested considerable time in learning about agriculture and agricultural technology, I recognized an opportunity to participate in an emerging industry.

## Bringing Technology Into Agriculture

At Big Whiskers, I developed an indoor growing facility and showroom where I cultivated hemp and worked with advanced agricultural lighting and controlled growing environments.

There was more to the venture than the crop itself.

There was also the technology associated with agriculture and the opportunity to demonstrate what that technology could accomplish.

I had already designed home aquaponics units and my friend Travis had designed units that were manufactured by a friend and mentor in China. Much of what I had learned through years of working with technology, developing systems, and conducting international business was applicable to the agricultural endeavors I was pursuing.

I hadn't left everything I knew behind when I set out to leave Gibson.

I was finding other ways to put that knowledge to work.

I also had an existing business to sustain. As I explained in the previous chapter, some of its established sources of revenue presented difficulties for me in light of my convictions about Scripture.

There were buildings to maintain, equipment to purchase, and improvements to make.

Developing new business opportunities was part of assuming responsibility for the property I had acquired.

## A Remarkable Contrast

Years earlier, I had spent approximately three years incarcerated following a cannabis possession case.

Those were the years during which I devoted so much of my time to studying Scripture.

Now, decades later, a change in federal law had presented me with an opportunity to pursue a business involving the same kind of plant.

I think the contrast is extraordinary.

But I don't want anyone to misunderstand what I was doing.

I hadn't decided to start growing marijuana for recreational use. I was pursuing a commercial hemp venture in response to a change in agricultural law.

Nor had I suddenly changed my understanding of cannabis because the government had changed its laws.

The change in the law had changed the business opportunities available to me.

The man considering those opportunities was a very different man from the one who had gone to prison years earlier.

Perhaps the most important difference was that I was now Daddy.

## Why I Was Doing It

Throughout these chapters, I have described a number of things that occupied my attention.

Technology.

Scripture.

Agriculture.

Business.

Community.

But I don't want the reader to lose sight of the reason I was trying to change the circumstances of my working life.

I loved my children more than anything.

I had spent years building a career that enabled me to provide for them. And I had reached a point at which I wanted to develop a way of providing for them that would also enable me to spend more time with them.

The farm was an opportunity to pursue that objective.

The hemp venture was one of the business opportunities I recognized in the course of doing so.

There is a difference between loving the work you do and loving the people for whom you do it.

I could become tremendously interested in the technology associated with a new agricultural industry. I could enjoy developing a business and learning about things I hadn't previously understood.

But those interests were not a replacement for my purpose as a father.

I didn't want to spend the rest of my life building things at the expense of being able to spend time with the people I loved.

I wanted the things I built to make that time possible.

The cannabis venture was an opportunity.

My children were my purpose.
