# Chapter Eight — Finding My People

I was trying to build a greenhouse for my family.

Of all the things that could have led to one of the most significant discoveries in my understanding of Scripture, I would not have expected that to be one of them.

But that is how I met Travis.

## Something I Had Yet to Learn

I had become interested in aquaponics, a way of growing plants and raising fish in a system in which the two benefit one another. In the process of researching how to build a greenhouse in my backyard, I found someone who had considerably more experience with these things than I did.

His name was Travis, and I eventually purchased about six thousand dollars' worth of equipment and components from him.

I knew I had things to learn from Travis about aquaponics.

What I didn't realize was how much I had to learn from him about the Bible.

By that time, conversations about Scripture had become a fairly ordinary part of my everyday life. I looked at the people I encountered as opportunities to share some of the things I had learned. I sincerely thought that meeting Travis would be another one of those opportunities.

He could help me build a greenhouse, and perhaps I could teach him some things about Scripture.

It didn't take me long to discover that Travis knew things about the Bible that I hadn't learned yet.

Now, I want you to understand what that meant to me.

I had spent years studying Scripture. During my incarceration, studying the Bible had occupied much of virtually every day. I had studied the languages, examined the relationships between passages, and wrestled with questions that I hadn't even known to ask when I first began reading the Bible.

And I hadn't stopped studying when I got out of prison.

I had spent years sharing what I had learned with other people, both in person and through the resources I was developing on the internet.

But all those years of study didn't mean that I had finished learning.

Travis helped remind me of that.

## Identity Crisis

One of the things Travis shared with me was a teaching by a pastor named Jim Staley. It was called *Identity Crisis*.

It is difficult for me to explain the significance of that teaching without also explaining a considerable amount of Scripture. But I don't want you to lose sight of the story while trying to understand all the theology behind it.

The question at the heart of the teaching was essentially this:

Who is Israel, and what does that have to do with those of us who follow Yeshua?

The Bible tells the story of a people with whom our Creator established a covenant. It tells us about the division of their kingdom, their disobedience, their scattering among the nations, and the promises of their restoration.

These were things I had studied.

I had also studied the words of Paul concerning Gentiles being grafted into an olive tree in Romans 11. I knew what he had written in Ephesians 2 about people who had once been aliens from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers from the covenants of promise being brought near through Messiah.

I knew what Galatians 3 said about those who belong to Messiah being Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise.

What I had not fully understood was how all of those things related to one another and, perhaps more importantly, how they related to me.

As I understood the teaching, the restoration promised in the prophets and the inclusion of people from the nations through Messiah were not disconnected stories.

They belonged to the same story.

I began to understand my relationship to Israel differently. I did not have to imagine myself as belonging to a people whose relationship with the Creator was somehow separate from the covenant story I had been reading throughout the Scriptures.

Through Messiah, I understood myself to have been brought into that story.

That is an important distinction. Paul doesn't describe the grafted branches as having established another tree. He also warns them against boasting over the other branches.

The point, as I came to understand it, was not that I had become more important than someone else. Nor was it that I had discovered some biological ancestry that I could prove.

It was that I had come to understand my identity in relation to the promises of our Creator and the people to whom those promises had been given.

I had already believed in Yeshua.

What changed was my understanding of who I was as a follower of Yeshua.

## Sharing What I Had Found

The teaching had such an impact on me that I shared it on Facebook.

I didn't particularly care what other people thought about my doing so. For the most part, I had never allowed the opinions of other people to determine what I was willing to study or share.

That didn't mean I was always right.

It meant that I wasn't willing to dismiss something simply because it might be unpopular.

And I wasn't going to dismiss something because it challenged my own understanding either.

There is a difference between accepting everything a teacher says and recognizing that a teacher has helped you see something you had previously missed. The authority remained Scripture. That was as important to me then as it is now.

What made this particular experience so significant was the way it brought together so much of what I had already studied.

Many other people have encountered that teaching and experienced a change in how they understand their identity. But I can only tell you what it meant to me, with the particular background I brought to it.

Years of studying Scripture had given me a great deal of material with which to examine what I was hearing.

And now I had even more reason to study.

## Finding My People

My journey toward fellowship had not been confined to the internet. Returning to church was part of that journey too.

Despite the difficulties I had experienced with churches after getting out of prison, I had never abandoned my belief in the importance of believers gathering together.

But through Travis, I began discovering an entire world of people I hadn't known existed.

Some would describe themselves as Messianic believers. Others would identify with what is commonly called the Hebrew Roots movement. Neither label has ever completely described me, and there is considerable diversity among the people who use those terms.

What mattered to me was discovering people who were asking many of the same questions I had been asking.

People who wanted to understand the Scriptures as a whole.

People who were willing to examine the relationship between the teachings of Messiah and the things our Creator had spoken through Moses and the prophets.

And people from whom I could learn.

For years, much of my study had been done independently. Now I was discovering opportunities to share the things I had learned with people who could also share things they had learned with me.

Those were some of the most exciting years of my life.

I traveled to conferences. I met authors whose books I had begun reading. I spent countless hours on the phone discussing Scripture with believers from around the world.

Some of those people stopped by our home while traveling through Tennessee. Others invited me to dinner when we happened to be in the same city.

The relationships were no longer confined to conversations on a computer.

They were becoming part of our lives.

## A Community Beyond My Own Home

As I became more involved, I learned that leaders from several of these communities had gathered in Jerusalem for an international congress.

By the time I reached out, I had also just missed a gathering at Henry Horton State Park, practically in my own backyard compared to the distances many of these people traveled.

I asked where the next gathering would take place.

Florida.

My wife and I decided to go.

There were hundreds of people from different backgrounds, cultures, and nations. The opportunity to gather with so many people who were interested in the same scriptural questions was extraordinary to me.

And it meant a great deal to me to be able to share that experience with my wife.

For years, many of the things I had been studying were things I had tried to explain at home. Now there was an opportunity for us to meet other people who were examining many of those same things.

Some of the friendships that developed during those years continued for a long time.

A few people eventually wrote books and sent me signed copies, acknowledging ideas that had emerged in the course of our conversations.

I had learned from them, and they had learned from me.

That is what I understand Proverbs 27:17 to mean when it speaks of iron sharpening iron.

It doesn't describe one piece of iron doing all the work.

## What We Have to Offer One Another

Around that period, I had released an early version of my Bible study application. Several churches expressed interest in having me develop applications for them.

Looking back, I recognize that some of my frustration with mainstream Christianity influenced the way I viewed those opportunities.

I still had things to learn about fellowship, just as I still had things to learn about Scripture.

Finding people who shared many of my convictions didn't mean that I should stop listening to people who didn't. And it didn't mean that everyone I met would agree with everything I believed.

I didn't want HomeGroups to become another place where people had to agree with a particular person before they could participate in the pursuit of truth.

If that had been the nature of my relationship with Travis, think about what I might have missed.

The person I expected to teach had become someone who taught me.

And the thing that brought us together wasn't even a Bible study.

It was a greenhouse.

In the previous chapter, I explained why I believed sustainable living belonged within a Scripture-based community. Here was an example from my own life of why those things belonged together.

I was trying to learn how to feed my family, and in the process I encountered someone who helped feed me spiritually.

The practical and spiritual aspects of our lives don't have to exist in separate worlds.

We can help one another build things, learn things, provide for our families, and understand Scripture. We can develop relationships in which the gifts of one person become a blessing to another.

And we can be willing to receive as well as give.

## Finding My Place

When I became a father, my understanding of the Father changed.

Years later, through a relationship that began with something I was doing for my children, my understanding of the Father's family changed.

Both discoveries became part of the way I understood the purpose of HomeGroups.

I wanted people to have opportunities to study the Scriptures for themselves, to discover their place within the story of those Scriptures, and to develop relationships with other people who were doing the same.

But I also wanted those relationships to extend beyond the study itself.

I had already begun to see what could happen when people shared their knowledge and resources, when conversations became friendships, and when friendships extended into our homes.

What might we accomplish if we could bring more of those things together in one place?

Not simply a place to talk about the things we believed, but a place to begin living them together.

That question would become increasingly important as I pursued another dream.

I wanted to buy a farm.
